# Axes

An **axis** is a named continuum with two ends someone could reasonably argue for. Three to five
of them bound the space. Fewer and the space is a point; more and nobody can hold it in their head.

The good axes are always project-specific — derived from the thesis and the anti-references, in
the product's own vocabulary. This library is for when the derived list comes out thin or generic,
and for checking that an axis you already named has real ends.

## The test

1. **Both ends are defensible.** If one end is "cluttered" and the other is "clean", it is not an
   axis, it is a preference wearing a costume. Rename the ends until a competent designer would
   pick either: "dense" ←→ "airy".
2. **The category has a default position on it.** Name it. If you cannot say where everyone else
   sits, the axis is not describing a real decision in this category.
3. **Moving along it changes the build.** An axis that produces the same components either way is
   decoration. Density changes the grid; register changes the copy *and* the type; ceremony
   changes the flow's step count.
4. **It is not already settled.** An axis the user has locked (platform, brand color, an
   accessibility floor) belongs in **Fixed**, not in the space.

## Library

Reach for these as prompts, not as a menu. Rename the ends into the product's language whenever
you can — a named end from the user's own world beats a generic one every time.

### What it is

- **Tool ←→ Environment.** Something you pick up to do one job, or somewhere you inhabit.
- **Instrument ←→ Appliance.** Rewards skill and repays practice, or works identically forever.
- **Document ←→ Application.** Read and referenced, or operated.
- **Single surface ←→ System.** One screen that does the thing, or a world with rooms.

### How it behaves

- **Ceremony ←→ Immediacy.** Confirms, reviews, and steps, or one irreversible-feeling action.
- **Guided ←→ Open.** The path is laid, or the user assembles their own.
- **Stateful ←→ Stateless.** Remembers you and accrues, or is the same every visit.
- **Foreground ←→ Ambient.** Demands attention while used, or lives at the edge and notifies.
- **Progressive ←→ Flat.** Reveals depth as competence grows, or shows everything at once.

### How it feels

- **Register: formal ←→ familiar.** Where the copy and the chrome sit. Drives voice as much as type.
- **Authority ←→ Companionship.** It knows and tells you, or it works alongside you.
- **Confident ←→ Careful.** Acts and offers undo, or asks first.
- **Restraint ←→ Exuberance.** How much the surface is allowed to perform.
- **Quiet ←→ Loud.** Note this is *not* the same as restraint: a quiet design can be extremely
  expressive, and a loud one can be disciplined.

### How it is made

- **Density: dense ←→ airy.** Information per screen. The axis most often decided by accident.
- **Ornament: structural ←→ decorated.** Whether beauty comes from the arrangement or is applied.
- **Native ←→ Bespoke.** Platform conventions honored, or a vocabulary of its own.
- **Static ←→ Motion-led.** Whether motion carries meaning or only smooths transitions.
- **Uniform ←→ Composed.** Every screen from the same kit, or each one art-directed.

### Who it is for

- **Novice-first ←→ Expert-first.** Optimized for the first hour, or for the thousandth.
- **Solo ←→ Social.** Whether other people are visible in the product.
- **Consumer ←→ Practitioner.** Whether using it is part of someone's job.

## Deriving better ones

The library covers the recurring decisions. The axis that makes the space *this project's* comes
from one of these moves:

- **Invert the anti-reference.** Name what the anti-reference is at one end, and the honest
  opposite at the other. Three anti-references usually yield one real axis between them.
- **Take the thesis's noun and find its opposite.** If the thesis claims "a workshop, not a
  gallery", then workshop ←→ gallery is an axis, and it is already in the user's language.
- **Find the argument.** Whatever the user has contradicted themselves on across the session is a
  live axis, not a mistake. Name it and hand it back.
- **Ask what the medium could be.** Print, terminal, instrument panel, board game, ledger, map,
  darkroom. Where the thing sits between two media is often the sharpest axis available.
